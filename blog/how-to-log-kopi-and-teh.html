How to Log Kopi and Teh: The Calories You Drink Before 9am

A kopi with condensed milk runs roughly 130–150 calories. Most people drink two or three a day and log exactly zero of them. Here's the kopitiam ordering ladder, the real numbers, and the five-second logging habit that fixes it.

5 min read · 21 July 2026

You log your chicken rice. You log your cai fan. You even log the bubble tea now. But the kopi you ordered at 7:45am, the teh after lunch, and the Milo at 4pm? Invisible. Not because you're hiding them — because they don't feel like food.

They add up anyway. Three sweetened kopitiam drinks a day can quietly land you 350–450 calories — more than a bowl of fishball noodle soup — without a single bite of anything.

The kopitiam menu, decoded by calories

The beautiful thing about kopitiam drinks is that the ordering language already encodes the calories. You just have to read it that way. Rough numbers for a standard cup:

OrderWhat's in itApprox. calories
KopiCoffee + condensed milk~130–150
Kopi CCoffee + evaporated milk + sugar~100–120
Kopi OBlack coffee + sugar~80–90
Kopi C kosongCoffee + evaporated milk, no sugar~40–50
Kopi O kosongBlack coffee, nothing added~5–10

Teh runs the same ladder, usually 10–20 calories higher per rung because tea takes more condensed milk to get the same colour. And Milo? A kopitiam Milo is 180–230 calories. Milo dinosaur, with the powder mountain on top, can clear 400.

The difference between kopi and kopi o kosong is about 140 calories. Order it twice a day and that's a kilogram of body weight every 25 days — decided entirely at the drinks stall.

Why nobody logs their kopi

Three reasons, all fixable:

  • It's automatic. You didn't decide to have kopi. It's just what happens when you sit down. Habitual consumption doesn't trigger the "should I log this?" reflex the way a plated meal does.
  • It feels too small to matter. One drink is small. But logging isn't about the one drink — it's about seeing the pattern. Three "too small to matter" drinks a day is the single most common gap we see between what people log and why their week didn't add up.
  • Searching a database for "kopi c siu dai" is miserable. Most Western tracking apps don't know what it is, or bury it under fourteen Starbucks entries. So people skip it once, then forever.

The five-second fix

You don't need to weigh anything or look anything up. When your kopi lands on the table, send the bot a voice note or a text: "kopi c". Done. KiloKaki knows the kopitiam menu — kopi, teh, Milo, and the whole modifier ladder: c, o, kosong, siu dai, gah dai, peng.

Iced versions log the same way — "teh peng" is still teh, just colder and slightly diluted. The calories barely change. What changes them is the milk and the sugar, and that's already in your order.

If you want to cut, walk down the ladder — don't jump

The classic mistake: going from kopi straight to kopi o kosong on a Monday morning, hating your life by Wednesday, and giving up by Friday. Bitterness tolerance is trainable, but not overnight.

Walk one rung at a time instead. Kopi → kopi siu dai (less sugar) → kopi c → kopi c siu dai → kosong if you ever feel like it. Each step saves 20–40 calories per cup and tastes only slightly different from the last. Give each rung two weeks. Your logs will show the trend line dropping without you ever feeling deprived.

And if you stay at kopi forever? Also fine. 140 calories is a perfectly reasonable thing to spend on something you drink every day and love. The point of logging isn't to shame you off condensed milk — it's to make the invisible visible, so the decision is actually yours.

The takeaway

  • Kopitiam drinks are the most under-logged calories in the Singapore diet — 350–450 a day for a three-cup habit.
  • The ordering language already tells you the calories: every rung down the ladder (c → o → kosong) saves 20–40 per cup.
  • Log the drink the moment it lands: one voice note, five seconds, no database search.
  • Cut one rung at a time, or don't cut at all — just know your number.

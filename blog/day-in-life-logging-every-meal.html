A Day in the Life: Logging Every Meal on KiloKaki for 24 Hours

46 seconds total. 5 meals. No app download, no barcode scanning, no manual entry.

I tracked everything I ate for 24 hours using only KiloKaki. No app download, no barcode scanning, no manual entry. Just photos and voice. Here's what actually happened — the good, the bad, and the surprising.

7:00 AM — Breakfast at the Hawker Centre

Meal: Kopi-O, 2 boiled eggs, 1 slice wholemeal toast
Log time: ~8 seconds

Walked into the kopitiam, ordered my usual. Pointed my phone at the tray, sent the photo to KiloKaki. Bot responded with:

Breakfast set (kopitiam style) Kopi-O: 45 cal · 2 boiled eggs: 155 cal · Wholemeal toast: 80 cal
Total: 280 cal

I didn't have to search for anything. The bot recognized it as a "kopitiam breakfast set" and broke it down automatically.

Friction check: Zero — I was still walking to my table when the macros came back.

12:30 PM — Lunch at Office Canteen

Meal: Chicken rice, extra cucumber, iced lemon tea
Log time: ~12 seconds

This is where most apps fail me. I open MyFitnessPal, search "chicken rice," get 14 community entries ranging from 320 to 680 calories. Which one is right? The one with 12 reviews? The one posted last week?

With KiloKaki: photo → done. Bot said:

Hainanese chicken rice (standard portion) Rice: 380 cal · Chicken (skin-on): 290 cal · Cucumber, sauce: 45 cal · Iced lemon tea: 90 cal
Total: 805 cal

I've logged this meal ~40 times across different apps. KiloKaki was the first time I didn't have to guess which entry matched my plate.

Friction check: Low — The only delay was waiting for the bot to process (~5 seconds).

4:00 PM — Afternoon Snack

Meal: Bubble tea (less sugar, jelly), 1 banana
Log time: ~6 seconds

This is the meal I usually skip logging. Why? Because logging a bubble tea means:

  1. Search brand (Tealive? Chatime? Tiger Sugar?)
  2. Pick size (medium? large?)
  3. Select sugar level (50%? 70%?)
  4. Add toppings (jelly? pearls? pudding?)
  5. Hope the entry matches what you actually got

KiloKaki: photo of the cup + banana. Bot said:

Bubble tea (50% sugar, jelly topping) + banana Bubble tea: 220 cal · Banana: 105 cal
Total: 325 cal

Friction check: Zero — This is the meal that usually falls through the cracks. If I can log it in 6 seconds, I will.

What I Learned

1. I logged everything. Every single thing.

This sounds trivial but it's not. On MyFitnessPal, I skip ~30% of meals. Not because I'm lying — because the friction is real. If logging takes 60+ seconds, I'll do it for big meals but skip the small stuff. The bubble tea. The late-night snack. The "it's just coffee" moment.

KiloKaki took 46 seconds total for 5 meals. That's 9 seconds per meal. I have no excuse.

2. The photo method is faster than searching, even when I know the name.

I've eaten chicken rice ~200 times. I know what's in it. I know roughly how many calories. But on MyFitnessPal I still have to: open app → search → scroll → select → confirm. That's 4 taps and 15-30 seconds of waiting.

KiloKaki is 1 tap + 1 photo + 5 seconds of processing. The difference compounds. Over a week, it's 5 minutes vs 25 minutes of logging.

3. I ate more than I thought.

2,705 calories. If I'd been estimating, I'd have said ~2,100. The gap wasn't "I was lying" — it was "I forgot to count things." The sauce on the chicken rice. The sugar in the kopi. The 3 satay sticks that turned out to be 210 calories (I estimated 120).

This is the underreporting problem. Lichtman et al. (NEJM 1992) found people underreport by 20-50%. I was underreporting by ~22% — right in the middle.

4. The bot isn't perfect, but it's consistent.

The chicken rice estimate (805 cal) is probably ±100 calories. I don't know exactly how much oil the auntie used, or whether the rice was cooked with extra butter. But the error is consistent — if it's 10% high today, it's 10% high tomorrow. For weight tracking, consistency matters more than precision.

⚠️ Calorie disclaimer: Photo-based macro estimation is not lab-grade. KiloKaki's estimates are typically within ±15% for standard portions. That's comparable to the margin of error in database entries (which vary by brand, preparation method, and portion size). The point isn't perfect accuracy — it's that you'll actually log consistently, and consistency beats precision for long-term results.

How to Log Durian: A Durian Season Survival Guide

Food Logging · July 2026

You smell it before you see it. The stall near the MRT has restocked, there's a queue forming at 9pm, and someone in the family group chat has already sent the photo: "MSW $12/kg!!" Durian season is in full swing — and if you log your food, you're about to face the most quietly unlogged food in Singapore.

Be honest: when was the last time durian appeared in your food log? Not the last time you ate it. The last time you logged it.

For most people the answer is never. Durian occupies a strange blind spot — too "healthy" to feel like dessert, too indulgent to log with a straight face. So it goes unrecorded, season after season, while the log stays clean and the data quietly stops matching reality.

Why nobody logs durian

The fruit exemption. Somewhere along the way, we all absorbed the rule that fruit doesn't count. Fruit is healthy. Fruit is what you eat instead of the bad stuff. And for most fruit, honestly, close enough — nobody's week was wrecked by papaya. Durian is the exception. It's the one fruit that eats like a dessert, and treating it like an apple is how a "light supper" becomes a mystery 800 calories.

The season amnesty. "It only comes once a year." (Twice, actually — but who's counting.) Scarcity feels like permission: the season is short, so the rules pause. Except the season runs for weeks, the stall is on your way home, and "once a year" somehow happens every few days through July.

The shame skip. This is the big one. You know durian is heavy. You suspect the number will be ugly. So you don't log it — not because you forgot, but because you'd rather not see it. That's not a durian problem; that's the log turning into a judge instead of a record. A food log you only fill in on good days isn't data. It's a highlight reel.

What durian actually costs (the honest math)

Here's the part nobody tells you, because durian doesn't come with a nutrition label:

  • Durian flesh runs about 147 kcal per 100 g. That's roughly triple papaya, and half as much again as banana. For fruit, that's dense.
  • One seed carries about 30–45 g of flesh — call it 50–70 kcal per seed. Mao Shan Wang seeds are often smaller but the flesh is richer and creamier, so the math lands in the same range.
  • A small box from the stall (4–6 seeds) is roughly 250–400 kcal. Kaya toast set territory. A real snack, not a rounding error — but nowhere near a catastrophe.
  • A proper session — the newspaper-on-the-table, whole-fruit, "just one more seed" kind — can hit 700–1,000 kcal. That's a full plate of chicken rice you didn't write down.

Why is durian the outlier? Fat. Most fruit is water and sugar. Durian carries around 5 g of fat per 100 g alongside its carbs — that famous custard texture is literal richness. It's the only common fruit with a dessert's macro profile.

And still: durian is real food. Fibre, potassium, vitamin C, B vitamins. This is not a lecture about avoiding it. It's the opposite — it's a case for writing it down like the real food it is.

The durian party problem

Even if you wanted to log durian properly, the setting fights you. Nobody eats durian off a labelled plate. You're standing at a roadside stall, or crowded around newspaper on a void deck table. The box is shared. You eat with your hands. Seeds disappear and no one is keeping score — least of all you.

This is the same trap as zi char and steamboat: shared, unstructured, unmeasurable. And the fix is the same. Stop trying to measure. Start counting.

The seed is the unit. You will never know the grams, and you don't need to. "I had four seeds" is all the precision durian requires. Four seeds ≈ 200–280 kcal. Done. That estimate — even if it's off by fifty calories — keeps your day's data honest and your streak alive.

How to actually log it

The whole flow takes ten seconds:

  1. Photo the open box before the first seed. One snap of the box (or the spread on the newspaper) gives you a record of what you started with. KiloKaki reads the photo and estimates.
  2. Or voice-log the seed count after. Sticky fingers and phones don't mix — durian is exactly what voice logging is for. When you're done and wiped down: "four seeds of mao shan wang." That's it. Logged.
  3. Log the session, not each seed. One entry: "durian, 6 seeds." Not six entries. The log should cost you less effort than the queue did.
  4. If you're not sure, estimate generously and move on. A logged guess beats an accurate blank every single time. The point isn't the perfect number — it's that tomorrow-you can see what actually happened.

Eat the durian

Let's be clear about the goal, because this is where food logging gets misunderstood. The log is not a permission system. You don't log durian to get approval to eat it. You log it because it happened, and because your data is only useful when it includes the things you actually ate — especially the delicious, creamy, 147-kcal-per-100-g things.

Durian season is short. A 300-calorie snack you loved, shared with your family, and wrote down in ten seconds? That's not a failure. That's a working system. What wrecks a week is never the durian — it's the "I've already blown it" spiral that follows an unlogged one, when one missing entry gives you permission to stop recording the rest of the day.

A seed of durian is 50–70 calories. Skipping the log doesn't make it zero — it just makes your data blind. Count seeds. Log the session. Enjoy the season.

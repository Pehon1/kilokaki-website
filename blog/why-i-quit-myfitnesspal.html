Why I Quit MyFitnessPal (And What I Use Instead)

Three years of logging. One redesign. Zero patience left.

KiloKaki · August 8, 2026

I spent three years logging my meals on MyFitnessPal. I loved it when it worked. And then, slowly, it didn't.

The redesign added more taps. The calorie info got smaller. The free features started disappearing behind a paywall. One day I sat down to log my lunch — a plate of chicken rice from the hawker centre downstairs — and realised I'd spent longer searching for the food than I had eating it.

That was the moment I quit.

The Problem With Calorie Apps (Especially in Singapore)

Every major food logging app was built for the same user: someone in the US or Europe, eating from a database of branded, barcoded products. Open a package. Scan the barcode. Done.

But try logging a plate of nasi lemak from a neighbourhood stall. Or cai fan from the canteen. Or char kway teow from the kopitiam uncle who doesn't weigh anything and definitely doesn't have a barcode.

There's no barcode for hawker food. There's no standardised entry for "half chicken, half pork, extra rice, less sauce." You end up guessing, estimating, and eventually giving up — which is exactly why most people quit food logging within three weeks.

Not because they don't want to track. Because the apps fight them every step of the way.

What I Needed Instead

I didn't need more features. I needed less friction.

Specifically:

  • Fast logging. If it takes more than 10 seconds, I won't do it consistently.
  • SG food understanding. I eat local. The app needs to know what nasi lemak is — not just "rice with chicken."
  • No app install. I'm already on my phone all day. I don't want another app to download, another account to manage, another subscription to forget about.
  • Photo or text. Sometimes I want to snap a picture. Sometimes I just want to type "nasi lemak" and be done.

I couldn't find an app that did all of this. So I started looking for alternatives.

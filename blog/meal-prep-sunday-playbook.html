The Sunday Meal-Prep Playbook: 90 Minutes, 5 Lunches, One Photo Per Meal

One shopping list, one Sunday session, five identical lunch boxes. Here's the exact playbook — including why boring is the point, and how it makes your food log nearly free for the whole week.

Most meal prep advice is written for people who like cooking. Three recipes, a marinade, a "flavour journey" across the week. It makes a great photo and a terrible habit, because by week two the Sunday session has quietly grown to three hours and you're back to the food court by Wednesday.

This playbook is the opposite. Ninety minutes, one recipe, five boxes. And it's built specifically for people who log their food — because the single biggest logging cost you pay all week is figuring out what you ate.

Meal prep deletes that cost. But only if you do it in a way that survives contact with an actual week.

Why five identical boxes (yes, really)

Every unique meal you eat has a logging cost: you have to identify it, estimate it, and enter it. Eat five different lunches and you pay that cost five times. Eat the same lunch five days and you pay it once — on Sunday, when the food is in front of you and the numbers are knowable.

That's the whole trick. Repetition isn't a compromise you accept to make meal prep work. Repetition is why meal prep works.

A food log only gets easier when your meals get more predictable. Meal prep is the only habit that does both at the same time.

The other benefit is decision fatigue. At 12:30pm on a Tuesday you are not making a good food decision — you're making a fast one. If the fast option is already in your bag, the decision was made on Sunday by the version of you who had time to think.

The shopping list

Everything below is in any Singapore supermarket — NTUC, Sheng Siong, Cold Storage, Giant. Nothing here needs a speciality store.

  • 1.2kg chicken thigh or breast — thigh stays juicier after reheating; breast if you want ~15 fewer kcal per 100g
  • 1kg broccoli (or any sturdy green: kale, chye sim, green beans)
  • 1kg rice — jasmine is fine; switch to brown if you want the fibre
  • 10 eggs
  • Oyster sauce, soy sauce, sesame oil, garlic — you probably already own these
  • 5 microwave-safe containers, 750ml–1L, ideally the same model

Total: roughly $18–25, or about $4–5 per lunch. Cheaper than the food court, and you know exactly what went in.

The 90-minute session

The timeline is staggered so nothing sits idle. Total hands-on time is closer to 40 minutes — the rest is the oven and rice cooker working while you wash up.

  1. 0:00 — Rice cooker on. 3 cups of rice, done in about 30 minutes. This is your base for all five boxes.
  2. 0:10 — Chicken in the oven. Tray, one layer, tossed with 1 tbsp oil, salt, garlic. 200°C for 25–30 minutes. No marinating overnight, no pan-searing in batches.
  3. 0:40 — Eggs on. Boil six minutes for soft, eight for hard. Ice bath, peel, two per box is a protein top-up for about 140 kcal.
  4. 0:50 — Greens. Blanch or steam the broccoli, three minutes max. Drain hard — watery greens are the number one reason prepped lunches get sad by day three.
  5. 1:10 — Pack. Rice first (one level scoop, roughly 200g cooked), chicken next (about 150–180g), greens to fill, two eggs on top.
  6. 1:25 — Log it. More on this below — it takes five minutes and it's the step everyone skips.

Done. Five boxes in the fridge, kitchen clean by 3pm on Sunday.

What's in a box

As packed above, each box lands around:

  • 550–650 kcal
  • 40–50g protein (chicken + two eggs)
  • 60–70g carbs (200g cooked rice)
  • 12–18g fat, depending on thigh vs breast and how heavy-handed you are with the sesame oil

That's a proper lunch — not a diet portion you'll be hunting snacks around at 3pm. If you need more or fewer calories, move one dial only: more rice for bulk days, skip the eggs for lighter days. Don't redesign the box.

The logging step everyone skips

Here's where this playbook earns its keep for people who track. On Sunday, while the food is literally on the scale in front of you, you log the meal once:

"Chicken 170g, rice 200g, broccoli 100g, 2 boiled eggs, 1 tsp sesame oil"

Say it to KiloKaki over voice, type it, or snap the open container — whichever takes you ten seconds. You now have an accurate, verified entry for a meal you're about to eat five times.

Monday to Friday, the entire logging workflow is: open box, take one photo, done. It's the same meal. The numbers were settled on Sunday by someone with a kitchen scale and nothing better to do. You don't re-estimate, you don't guess, you don't squint at a menu database for "chicken with rice and vegetables, homemade, unspecified, good luck."

The goal isn't a perfect log. It's a log so cheap to maintain that you never have a reason to stop.

If you've ever abandoned logging because estimating felt pointless, this is the fix: stop estimating at mealtime entirely. Move the estimating to the one moment when you actually have the information.

How to not get sick of it by Thursday

Identical boxes work — until flavour fatigue kills the habit. The fix is the three-sauce rotation: the base (rice, chicken, greens, eggs) never changes, so the logging stays free. Only the sauce changes, and sauce is calorically minor.

  • Boxes 1–2: oyster sauce + white pepper (the classic)
  • Boxes 3–4: soy sauce + sesame oil + a little chilli padi
  • Box 5: anything you'd put on cai fan — curry sauce, sambal, ginger spring onion

Same 600 calories, three different lunches as far as your brain is concerned. Log the base once, note the sauce if you're being thorough — a tablespoon of any of these is 15–40 kcal, which is rounding error against a 600 kcal meal.

Where this playbook usually dies (and how to not)

Death #1: Week-one ambition. People prep five different recipes, spend three hours, and never prep again. One recipe. Always one recipe. Add a second one only after week three.

Death #2: No containers on Sunday. If you have to wash or buy containers mid-session, you'll "do it next week." Buy the five identical containers once. They're infrastructure, like the rice cooker.

Death #3: Skipping the Sunday log. Without it, Monday-you is back to guessing what Sunday-you put in the box, and by Wednesday the data is guesswork and you stop logging. Five minutes on Sunday buys five days of clean data. This is the entire deal.

Death #4: Treating one skipped day as failure. Wednesday lunch happens at a client meeting. Fine — log the cai fan you ate, move on. The playbook resumes Thursday. Streaks survive gaps; they die from all-or-nothing thinking.

Your first session, this Sunday

  1. Buy the list. Five containers if you don't have them.
  2. Set 90 minutes. Start the rice cooker at minute zero.
  3. Pack five boxes. Weigh the first one if you have a scale; after that, the scoop is the measure.
  4. Log the meal once — photo, voice, or text.
  5. Put one box in tomorrow's bag before you go to bed.

Ninety minutes of work. Five lunches that cost you ten seconds each to log. That's the playbook — and it compounds. Every week you keep it up, your food log gets more accurate while taking less effort, which is the exact opposite of how dieting usually feels.

Log your five boxes once, on Sunday. Then just take photos all week.

How to Log Alcohol Without Wrecking Your Week

Beer, wine, cocktails — and the supper after. Alcohol is the most under-logged thing in your day. Here's how to track it without losing your mind, or your weekend.

You logged every meal Monday to Friday. Then Friday night happened — three beers, a plate of satay, and a teh tarik on the way home. None of it made it into your log.

Alcohol is the single most under-logged thing in most people's diaries. Not because it's hard to track. Because we don't want to see the number.

But the calories don't care whether you logged them. The week happened either way. So here's the honest, no-shame approach to logging drinks — built for how people actually drink in Singapore.

Why Alcohol Breaks Your Food Log

Three things make drinks the hardest thing to track:

1. It comes in rounds. You don't decide "I'll have two beers" and stop. Someone buys a round. Then you buy one back. By the end of the night you genuinely can't remember if it was three or five.

2. The mixers hide the damage. A vodka soda is about 100 calories. A Long Island iced tea is closer to 400 — most of it sugar. The alcohol is only half the story; the sweet stuff is the rest.

3. Drinking turns off logging. A few drinks in, the last thing you're doing is opening an app and searching a database. So the night becomes a blank in your log — and a blank reads as zero, which is a lie.

This is the same problem as the snack gap: the calories you don't log don't disappear, they just stop being visible. And invisible calories are exactly the ones that quietly stall your week.

The Drink Calorie Cheat Sheet

You don't need a database. You need a rough number you can log in five seconds. Here are sensible estimates for standard servings — round up if the pour was generous (it usually was).

Quick Reference — Per Standard Serving
  • Beer, regular (1 pint / 500ml)200–250 kcal
  • Beer, can (330ml)140–160 kcal
  • Tiger / lager (1 bottle)150–180 kcal
  • Red or white wine (175ml)150–180 kcal
  • Prosecco / sparkling (125ml)90–120 kcal
  • Spirit + soda (1 shot)90–110 kcal
  • Whisky / gin, neat (1 shot)95–110 kcal
  • Gin & tonic170–220 kcal
  • Cocktail, sweet (margarita, mojito)250–350 kcal
  • Long Island iced tea350–450 kcal
  • Soju (1 bottle, shared)540 kcal total
  • Sake (1 small carafe, 180ml)230–260 kcal

Note: These are estimates for standard pours. Bars and supper spots almost always pour heavier than a measured serving — when in doubt, log the bigger number.

The "Count the Glasses" Method

Forget grams and ABV maths. The fastest way to log a night out is to count glasses.

Step 1: Pick your default drink. If you mostly drink beer, your default is "1 beer ≈ 200 kcal." If it's wine, "1 glass ≈ 170 kcal." If it's spirits with mixer, "1 drink ≈ 200 kcal." One number, memorised.

Step 2: Count, don't measure. At the end of the night — or the next morning — you ask one question: how many glasses? Four beers is roughly 800 calories. You don't need to know the brewery. You need the ballpark.

Step 3: Log it as one entry. With KiloKaki you just say it: "four beers and some satay last night." Voice or text, one message, done. No searching, no per-item entry.

Counting glasses gets you 90% of the way there. Trying to be exact gets you to "ugh, forget it" — which logs nothing at all.

Don't Forget the Second Meal

Here's the part people miss entirely: the drinks are rarely the biggest number of the night. The supper is.

Alcohol lowers your guard and raises your appetite. That's biology, not weakness. So the prata at 1am, the bak chor mee after clubbing, the McDelivery you ordered "to soak it up" — that's often 600–900 calories sitting on top of the drinks. The same way food delivery quietly undoes a good week, the post-drinks meal is where a night out really lands.

Log the supper too. A photo is enough — snap the plate before you demolish it, add "after drinks," move on. This is exactly the photo-first approach that works on any meal, label or no label.

The Morning-After Backfill

Nobody logs accurately at 2am. So don't try. Do the morning-after backfill instead.

When you wake up, before the details fade, send one message: "Last night: ~4 beers, 1 stick satay set, supper was prawn mee." Two minutes. The whole night captured, roughly.

This works the same way eyeballing portions works — you're not aiming for lab precision, you're aiming for a number that's honest enough to be useful. A rough log beats a perfect blank every single time.

How to Drink and Still Hit Your Goals

Logging alcohol isn't about quitting. It's about seeing it clearly so you can make trades you actually choose:

  • Bank a little earlier in the day. If you know Friday drinks are coming, keep lunch lighter and protein-forward. You're spending from a calorie budget, not breaking rules.
  • Switch to lower-cost drinks mid-session. Spirit + soda instead of a third cocktail saves 200+ calories with zero loss of fun.
  • Alternate with water. Slows the pace, shrinks the count, and softens the morning. Your log and your head both thank you.
  • Pre-decide the supper. Deciding "one prata, not three" while sober beats deciding it while three beers in.

Can't remember the night? Backfill it in the morning. Rough beats blank.

The Real Point of Logging Drinks

When you start logging alcohol — even roughly — you stop being surprised. The "I ate so well this week, why isn't the scale moving?" mystery usually has a simple answer hiding in two big weekend nights. Logging makes it visible. Visible is what changes behaviour.

You don't have to drink less to log it. But once you log it, you'll probably want to make a few small trades — not out of guilt, just because now you can see what they cost. That's the whole game: awareness, not perfection.

So next Friday: count the glasses, snap the supper, backfill in the morning. Honest and rough wins.
